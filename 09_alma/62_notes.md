### Alma 62:1–11 — Moroni Rushes to the Aid of Pahoran in Gideon

Holding no grudges, Captain Moroni immediately responded to Pahoran's plea. Leaving his generals Lehi and Teancum in command of the northeastern front, Moroni marched with a small contingent toward the land of Gideon, gathering thousands of volunteers along the way under the Title of Liberty.

Upon uniting with Pahoran in Gideon, their combined forces marched on Zarahemla:
- **Pachus Defeated**: The rebel leader Pachus was slain in battle.
- **King-Men Tried**: The rebellious royalists were given a lawful trial; those who refused to take up arms in defense of their country were executed according to law.
- **Supplies Restored**: Essential armaments and grain stores in the capital were quickly secured and mobilized.

### Alma 62:12–30 — Relief to the Frontiers and the Recapture of Nephihah

With Zarahemla liberated, Moroni and Pahoran dispatched urgent provisions and reinforcements to Helaman on the southwestern front and to Lehi and Teancum on the eastern borders.

#### Strategic Settlement of Prisoners in Jershon
Willing Lamanite captives were settled in the land of **Jershon** (Alma 62:17, 27). Joining the peaceful Ammonites, they cultivated crops and supplied provisions to the Nephite armies, turning former enemies into productive, covenant-aligned contributors to society.

#### The Nighttime Recapture of Nephihah
Moroni then marched to retake the heavily fortified city of **Nephihah** (62:21–26). Demonstrating his tactical genius, Moroni sent men with ropes and ladders up the back wall where the Lamanites did not post guards because of the natural difficulty of the terrain. The Nephite soldiers climbed over the wall in the dead of night, took position inside the city, and overwhelmed the garrison at dawn without the loss of a single Nephite life (62:23).

### Alma 62:31–39 — The Death of Teancum and the End of the War

Moroni drove the remaining Lamanite armies southward from the land of Lehi to the land of Moroni, encamping around their perimeter (62:30–32).

At night, the daring hero **Teancum**—who had previously assassinated Amalickiah (Alma 51:34)—crept into the enemy camp once more. He found King Ammoron and threw a javelin that pierced him near the heart (Alma 62:35–37). However, before dying, Ammoron awoke his servant, who pursued Teancum and killed him.

When Moroni and Lehi discovered Teancum's body the next morning, they were filled with profound sorrow for their faithful, valiant friend. Teancum had fought with extraordinary courage for his country and his God, though his final act was fueled by personal anger and daring foolhardiness.

Following Ammoron’s death, the leaderless Lamanite army surrendered, and the devastating fourteen-year war of Amalickiah and Ammoron finally came to an end (62:38–39).

### Alma 62:41 — Hearts Softened vs. Hardened by Affliction

As you reflect on this long campaign, you might wonder what lessons can be drawn from building character through intense adversity:

> “But behold, because of the exceedingly great length of the war between the Nephites and the Lamanites many had become hardened, because of the exceedingly great length of the war; and many were softened because of their afflictions, insomuch that they did humble themselves before God, even in the depth of humility.” (Alma 62:41)

This profound observation is one of Mormon’s central theological takeaways from the entire war narrative. The same trials that harden some souls into cynicism and bitterness will soften and sanctify others in true humility before God.

### Alma 62:42–52 — Post-War Recovery and Leadership Transitions

Following the establishment of peace, a sudden and rapid succession of leadership crises overtook the Nephite nation:
- **Captain Moroni’s Retirement and Death**: Moroni yielded up the command of his armies to his son Moronihah and retired to his home. He died only two years later (Alma 62:43; 63:3). Was his death premature? Was it the result of untreated war injuries, disease, or the immense physical and emotional trauma of commanding through fourteen years of total war?
- **Helaman’s Regulation and Death**: Helaman returned to his sacred calling, regulated the church throughout the land, and died only one year later (62:45–52).
- **Pahoran’s Passing**: Pahoran returned to the judgment seat but would die only four years later (62:44; Helaman 1:1), leaving an unresolved succession contest between his sons that would spark new assassinations and civil strife.

The heavy casualties sustained during the war years, coupled with the rapid passing of its foundational military, political, and spiritual leaders, left a profound leadership vacuum that set the stage for the rise of the secret combinations in the book of Helaman.
