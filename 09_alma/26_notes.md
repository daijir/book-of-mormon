### Alma 26:1–9, 11–37 — Ammon Expresses Great Joy at the Success of Their Mission

With one brief interruption from Aaron who thinks that Ammon’s joy carries him “away unto boasting” (26:10), this entire chapter gives us “the words of Ammon to his brethren.” He expresses amazement at the great blessings the Lord had granted them during their fourteen-year mission, things they could not have imagined when they left their home and royal stations in Zarahemla.

#### Ammon's Detailed Reminiscences

Ammon's reminiscence is remarkably detailed:
- **Persecution upon Departure**: How they had been persecuted and laughed to scorn as they left (26:23; see Mosiah 27:32).
- **Divine Comfort and Promise**: How the Lord had comforted them and promised them success (26:27; see Alma 17:10–11).
- **Royal Protection**: Recalling the houses they entered, expressly protected under the proclamation of the king of the Lamanites (26:28; see Alma 23:2).
- **Instruments of God**: Becoming “instruments” in the hands of the Lord (26:3, 15; see Alma 17:9).
- **Thousands Gathered**: The thousands who now rejoice in the fold of God (26:4).
- **Sufferings Endured**: In spite of all their great sufferings (26:28, 34; see Alma 21:14).
- **Patience in Affliction**: Being “patient in afflictions” (26:27, 28; see Alma 17:11).

In every respect, the rejoicing of Ammon in Alma 26 acknowledges the fulfillment of the blessings given to the sons of Mosiah at the outset of their mission. Thus, Aaron may have been a little too quick in objecting to Ammon’s exuberance. He rejoiced as he glorified God, not himself (26:11–12, 35–36). We might well wonder if we rejoice in Ammon’s way often enough today.

#### Verbal Allusions to Alma's Angelic Conversion

In reading Ammon’s words, an interesting personal thread also runs through his exuberant reverie, namely the number of times his words echo the words of Alma, as he described the appearance of the angel to him and the four sons of Mosiah reported in Mosiah 27 or other words of Alma about their conversion. Take note of the following distinct verbal allusions:
- **Darkest abyss**: Alma 26:3 (recalling Mosiah 27:29)
- **Marvelous light**: Alma 26:3 (recalling Mosiah 27:29)
- **Pains of hell**: Alma 26:13 (see Alma 36:13)
- **Sing the song of redeeming love**: Alma 26:13 (see Alma 5:9)
- **Snatched**: Alma 26:17 (recalling Mosiah 27:28)

#### Echoes of King Benjamin’s Speech

Remember also that these sons of Mosiah were grandsons of King Benjamin. Thus, be on the lookout for places in Ammon’s rejoicing that are drawn from King Benjamin’s foundational speech:
- **Natural man**: Alma 26:21 (see Mosiah 3:19)
- **Know the mysteries of God**: Alma 26:22 (see Mosiah 2:9)
- **Divine Omniscience and Omnipotence**: “God has all power, all wisdom, all understanding, and comprehendeth all things” (Alma 26:35; see Mosiah 4:9)

#### Hebrew Numerology: Joy to the Seventh Power

Finally, notice that the word **“joy”** appears in Ammon’s ecstatic reflection exactly **seven times**:

| # | Verse Reference | Phrase in Text |
| :-: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Alma 26:11 | “I do not boast in my own strength, nor in my own wisdom; but behold, **my joy** is full...” |
| **2** | Alma 26:11 | “...yea, my heart is brim with joy, and I will rejoice in my God.” |
| **3** | Alma 26:16 | “Therefore, let us glory, yea, we will glory in the Lord; yea, we will rejoice, for **our joy** is full...” |
| **4** | Alma 26:30 | “And we have suffered all manner of afflictions, and all this, that perhaps we might be the means of saving some soul; and we supposed that **our joy** would be full if perhaps we could be the means of saving some.” |
| **5** | Alma 26:35 | “Now have we not reason to rejoice?

Yea, I say unto you, there never were men that had so great reason to rejoice as we, since the world began; yea, and **my joy** is carried away...” |
| **6** | Alma 26:36 | “...and this is **my joy**, and my great thanksgiving; yea, and I will give thanks unto my God forever. Amen.” |
| **7** | Alma 26:37 | “Now my brethren, we see that God is mindful of every people... and **my joy** is full.” |

Another seven-fold expression of complete joy will show up again in **Alma 27:17–19**, and yet a third time in **Alma 29:5, 9, 10, 13, 14, 14, 16**. These triple expressions of joy to the seventh power, along with the many precise word choices in these deeply personal chapters, seem far too literarily purposeful and symbolically meaningful to be accidental or unintentional.
