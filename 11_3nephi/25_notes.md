### 3 Nephi 25 (Malachi 4) — The Sun of Righteousness and the Mission of Elijah

In 3 Nephi 25, Jesus concludes His quotation of the prophecies of Malachi by delivering what is known in the Old Testament as Malachi 4. Because the Nephites had left Jerusalem around 600 BC—long before Malachi lived and wrote (ca. 430 BC)—the Nephite records did not contain these crucial revelations concerning the last days and the final judgment. Jesus specifically commanded that these words be written into their permanent scriptural record (3 Nephi 24:1; 26:1–2).

---

### Temple Elements in 3 Nephi 25

As John W. Welch and Marshall Goodrich note, Jesus delivered these passages at the Nephite temple in Bountiful in the presence of parents and their children. The imagery and promises in 3 Nephi 25 correspond directly to sacred temple themes:

| Verse | Prophetic Imagery | Temple / Covenant Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **25:1** | The day cometh that shall burn as an oven; proud and wicked burned as stubble | Final judgment; sanctification of the earth from evil; leaving neither root nor branch |
| **25:2** | “Unto you that fear my name, shall the Son of Righteousness arise with healing in his wings” | The healing power of Christ’s Atonement; the rising Sun/Son; righteousness growing up as calves of the stall |
| **25:4** | “Remember ye the law of Moses my servant ... with the statutes and judgments” | Sacred obedience to eternal laws and covenant statutes given at Mount Sinai (Horeb) |
| **25:5** | “Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day” | Restoration of the sealing keys of the holy priesthood |
| **25:6** | “He shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers” | Eternal family organization; vicarious temple ordinances binding generations together lest the earth be smitten with a curse |

### The Bookends of the Restoration: From Bountiful to Palmyra

When the resurrected Savior concluded his second day of teaching at the temple in Bountiful, He brought His scripture additions and Covenant Sermon to a climax with Malachi 4:5–6.

Remarkably, when the angel Moroni appeared to the young Prophet Joseph Smith on the night of September 21, 1823, Moroni quoted this exact prophecy with slight variations (see D&C 2:1–3; Joseph Smith—History 1:38–39):

> “Behold, I will reveal unto you the Priesthood, by the hand of Elijah the prophet, before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord. And he shall plant in the hearts of the children the promises made to the fathers, and the hearts of the children shall turn to their fathers. If it were not so, the whole earth would be utterly wasted at his coming.”

Moroni began the opening work of the Restoration in 1823 precisely where Jesus Christ had left off at the end of Day 2 in Bountiful. The dispensation of the fulness of times is fundamentally anchored in the fulfillment of this promise through the return of Elijah (fulfilled on April 3, 1836, in the Kirtland Temple; D&C 110:13–16).

### Turning the Hearts of the Fathers and Children

Just as Jesus had ended Day 1 by focusing on the family (blessing the little ones in 3 Nephi 17:23 and commanding family prayer in 18:21), He concludes Day 2 by delivering Malachi’s promise that generation will be bound to generation.

The covenant of God is both a cosmic covenant of gathering and an intimate family covenant. In ancient and modern temples alike, the turning of hearts bridges the divide between ancestors and posterity, ensuring that God's children are eternally sealed and gathered into His presence.
