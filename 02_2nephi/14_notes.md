### 2 Nephi 14 (Isaiah 4) in the Nephite Prophetic View

2 Nephi 14 continues the prophetic discourse of Isaiah 3–4, moving from the judgment and social collapse of Jerusalem to the divine promise of sanctuary and holiness.

#### Stage 2: Warfare and Desolation in Judah (2 Nephi 14:1)
The opening verse concludes the severe judgment pronounced in chapter 13 upon the proud men and women of Jerusalem:
> “And in that day, seven women shall take hold of one man, saying: We will eat our own bread, and wear our own apparel; only let us be called by thy name to take away our reproach.” (2 Nephi 14:1)

Due to the devastating casualties of war, the normal social and familial fabric of society has dissolved. The women are willing to provide their own food and clothing contrary to ancient marriage covenants (Exodus 21:10), desiring only the legal protection of marriage to escape social dishonor.

#### Stage 4B: The Branch of the Lord and Holy Remnant (2 Nephi 14:2–4)
Immediately following the tragic imagery of verse 1, Isaiah shifts to glorious restoration:
- **The Branch of the Lord**: “In that day shall the branch of the Lord be beautiful and glorious, the fruit of the earth excellent and comely to them that are escaped of Israel” (14:2).
- **The Holy Remnant**: “He that is left in Zion and remaineth in Jerusalem shall be called holy, every one that is written among the living in Jerusalem” (14:3).
- **Spiritual Cleansing**: The filth of the daughters of Zion is washed away by the spirit of judgment and burning (14:4).

#### Stage 4B: The Temple Tabernacle, Cloud, and Fire (2 Nephi 14:5–6)
Isaiah invokes the sacred wilderness sanctuary and Mount Sinai presence:
> “And the Lord will create upon every dwelling-place of mount Zion, and upon her assemblies, a cloud and smoke by day and the shining of a flaming fire by night; for upon all the glory of Zion shall be a defence. And there shall be a tabernacle for a shadow in the daytime from the heat, and for a place of refuge, and a covert from storm and from rain.” (2 Nephi 14:5–6)

In this promise, every home and gathering place of the righteous becomes endowed with the same divine protection and presence that anciently rested over the holy tabernacle.
