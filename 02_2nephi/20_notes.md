### 2 Nephi 20 (Isaiah 10) in the Nephite Prophetic View

In 2 Nephi 20, Isaiah delivers his famous indictment of the mighty Assyrian empire. In God’s overarching plan of history, Assyria was merely an instrument—the rod of God’s anger—used to chastise an unfaithful covenant people. However, when the king of Assyria boasted that his victories were due to his own prowess and wisdom, the Lord decreed the utter desolation of the empire.

#### Stage 4A: The Boasting Tool and Judgment on the Oppressor (2 Nephi 20:1–19)
Isaiah exposes the folly of prideful tyranny:
- **Unrighteous Decrees Rebuked**: “Wo unto them that decree unrighteous decrees, and that write grievousness which they have prescribed; to turn away the needy from judgment, and to take away the right from the poor of my people” (20:1–2).
- **The Axe Boasting against the Hewer**:
  > “Shall the ax boast itself against him that heweth therewith? Shall the saw magnify itself against him that shaketh it? As if the rod should shake itself against them that lift it up, or as if the staff should lift up itself as if it were no wood!” (2 Nephi 20:15)
- **The Consuming Fire**: The Lord sends leanness into his fatness, and the Light of Israel shall be for a fire that burns and devours his thorns and briers in one day (20:16–17).

#### Stage 3: The Remnant of Israel Shall Return (2 Nephi 20:20–23)
Amidst international upheaval, the covenant promise of gathering shines forth:
- **True Trust**: Israel will no longer stay upon the foreign superpowers that smote them, but will “stay upon the Lord, the Holy One of Israel, in truth” (20:20).
- **The Holy Remnant**: “The remnant shall return, yea, even the remnant of Jacob, unto the mighty God” (20:21). Even though the people of Israel were anciently as the sands of the sea, a righteous remnant will be preserved and redeemed (20:22).

#### Stage 4A: Divine Deliverance and the Felling of Haughty Boughs (2 Nephi 20:24–34)
The Lord comforts His covenant people in Jerusalem:
- **Comfort in Zion**: “O my people that dwellest in Zion, be not afraid of the Assyrian” (20:24), for within a very little while the indignation will cease.
- **The Dramatic Advance Halted**: Isaiah maps the rapid military approach of the Assyrian army marching south toward Jerusalem—passing through Aiath, Migron, Michmash, Ramah, Gibeah, Gallim, and Anathoth (20:28–31). The invader halts at Nob, shaking his fist against Mount Zion (20:32).
- **Sudden Overthrow**: At the gates of the holy city, the Lord of Hosts lops the bough with terror; the high ones of stature are hewn down, and Lebanon falls by a mighty one (20:33–34), foreshadowing the miraculous destruction of Sennacherib’s army outside Jerusalem.
